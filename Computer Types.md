
# Types of Computers

## Overview

In this room, I covered the different types of computers used in everyday life, business environments and IT infrastructure. The focus was on understanding how computers differ depending on their intended purpose, from everyday personal devices to servers and computers built into other equipment.

## Task 1 – Introduction

The room introduced the different ways computers are used beyond traditional desktops and laptops.

Computing devices are now found in everyday objects such as refrigerators, doorbells, coffee machines and thermostats. Many of these devices contain processors and other computing components that allow them to carry out specific functions, with some also connecting to the internet.

## Task 2 – The Computers You Sit in Front Of

This section covered four main types of computers and the differences between them.

**Laptop**

Designed for portability and everyday use. Laptops combine the screen, keyboard, battery and internal hardware into one device. Their compact design means they generally have more limitations when it comes to cooling and sustained performance.

**Desktop**

Designed for use in a fixed location. Desktops generally have more room for internal components, better cooling capabilities and the ability to maintain performance over longer periods.

**Workstation**

Built for professional workloads where performance, precision and reliability are important. These systems are commonly used for engineering, simulations, 3D modelling and other demanding tasks.

**Server**

Designed to provide services, resources and data to other devices over a network. Unlike personal computers, servers typically operate without a dedicated screen or keyboard and can handle requests from multiple users simultaneously.

## Task 3 – Computers Beyond the Desktop

This section moved away from traditional computers and looked at devices that I use or encounter regularly but don't necessarily think of as computers.

**Smartphones**

Pocket-sized computers designed around portability, battery life and connectivity. They combine processing power, storage, an operating system and networking capabilities.

**Tablets**

Touchscreen-based computers with larger displays than smartphones, commonly used for browsing, productivity and media.

**IoT Devices**

Internet of Things devices connect to a network to exchange information, report data or receive commands.

Examples include smart thermostats, doorbells, fitness trackers and connected household appliances.

**Embedded Computers**

These are computers built into other devices to control specific functions. They are often designed to operate independently with little or no user interaction.

Examples include coffee machine controllers, automatic door sensors and lighting systems.

### IoT vs Embedded Systems

An important distinction covered here was the difference between IoT devices and embedded computers.

IoT devices rely on network connectivity to communicate or exchange information. Embedded computers don't necessarily require a network connection and can simply perform their dedicated function within the device they're built into.

## Task 4 – Choosing the Right Computer

The final section brought the different computer types together and explained why there isn't one computer that is suitable for every purpose.

Different designs involve different trade-offs:

- Mobility affects available power, cooling and sustained performance.
- Workstations prioritise performance, accuracy and reliability.
- Servers are designed to handle multiple users and requests, often using redundant components to reduce downtime.
- IoT and embedded devices are built around specific functions and requirements.

The main point was that computer hardware and design depend on what the system is expected to do.
